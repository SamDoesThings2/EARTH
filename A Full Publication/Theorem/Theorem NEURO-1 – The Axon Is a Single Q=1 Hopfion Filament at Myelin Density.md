## Physical Interpretation

The nervous system is not a network of wires carrying electrical signals. It's a lattice of Hopfion filaments supporting topological solitons.

**Why axons have a characteristic diameter:** The 1.139 μm inner diameter is not an optimized design but a topological requirement. **Why myelination increases conduction speed:** Myelin doesn't "insulate" the axon in the conventional sense. It sets the density that determines ξ_axon. The nodes of Ranvier are not gaps in insulation but resonant points in the φ³ spacing pattern where kinks can be amplified.

**Why unmyelinated axons are slower:** Without myelin, the effective density is different, changing ξ. This changes both the filament diameter and the kink propagation speed. Unmyelinated fibers in mammals conduct at ~0.5–2 m/s, consistent with different ξ values at non-myelin densities.

**Why the all-or-nothing principle:** Kinks are topologically protected objects. They either exist with full rest mass (1.03 MeV) or don't exist at all. There's no partial kink, just as there's no partial proton.

**Why action potentials don't attenuate:** Electrical signals decay with distance due to resistance and capacitance. But topological solitons maintain their shape indefinitely because they're solutions to the nonlinear wave equation. The kink that arrives at the axon terminal is identical to the one initiated at the axon hillock.

**Why there's a refractory period:** After a kink passes, the filament is in an excited state. It must relax back to the ground state (time ~ 1/√λ̃) before another kink can form. This sets the absolute refractory period and limits firing rates.

**Why nerve damage is permanent:** If the Hopfion filament is severed (axotomy), the topological charge is no longer conserved along its length. **Why different species have different conduction speeds:** Species variation in nerve conduction is not about evolution optimizing diameter or myelination thickness. It's about different myelin densities producing different ξ values. Large mammals tend to have denser myelin → smaller ξ → faster conduction.

**Why squid giant axons are giant:** Squid lack myelin. To achieve fast conduction speeds needed for escape responses, they must use unmyelinated axons with very large diameters (~0.5–1 mm). The larger diameter corresponds to a different coherence regime where ξ can support faster kinks despite the absence of saltatory amplification.
