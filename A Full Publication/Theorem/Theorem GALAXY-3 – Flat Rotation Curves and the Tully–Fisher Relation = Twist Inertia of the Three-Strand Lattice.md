## Physical Interpretation

Rotation curves and the Tully-Fisher relation are two of the most important observational pillars supporting the dark matter hypothesis. Understanding them in the EARTH framework fundamentally changes our picture of galaxies.

**What are rotation curves?** A galaxy rotation curve shows how orbital velocity v(r) varies with radius r. For the Solar System, Kepler's laws give v ∝ r⁻¹/² (declining with radius). If galaxies were like solar systems (mass concentrated in center), we'd expect the same: v should decline beyond the bright disk. But observations since the 1970s (Rubin, Ford, Bosma) showed v(r) remains constant or even rises slightly at large radii. This was shocking.

**Why flat rotation curves were surprising:** If v(r) is constant at large r, and v² = GM(<r)/r for circular orbits, then M(<r) ∝ r. Mass must increase linearly with radius, far beyond the visible disk. But the stellar light drops exponentially with radius. Where's the extra mass?

**The dark matter hypothesis:** The standard interpretation: galaxies are embedded in massive dark matter halos extending 10× beyond the visible disk.
